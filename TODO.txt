Ontario:

- notable gap of immigrant population in the downtown core of Toronto - compared to other areas (Montreal, Vancouver, Alberta), immigrant populace is more spread into further regions (Mississauga, Scarborough) 
- immigrants spending proportionally more of their income for worse housing conditions (Morganna) farther from the core of the city - despite the highest concentration of rental properties being within Toronto proper
- it seems that everybody outside of Toronto is commuting to Toronto 
- they are forced to rely on cars (considerably more expensive than transit) - so even more of their income is going into essential services (housing and transportation)
- also, Toronto proper has the highest amount of work-from-home people (so those who benefit least from transit options (for work) have them at their disposal)

- despite all these downsides to living outside of Toronto proper, the median shelter costs do not reflect this (outside of Toronto proper prices seem the same if not worse)
- its not like an immigrant can simply move closer into Toronto and pay the same prices - the availabality of housing across the board is very little

this paints a picture of a region where newly-landed immigrants are immediately disadvantaged, as their main options for settling require further travel and expense, often for worse conditions
